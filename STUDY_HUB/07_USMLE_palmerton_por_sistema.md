## PART 1 — Palmerton's GENERAL METHOD (verified vs unverified)

| Element | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Pathogenesis-to-presentation cards** | Each card connects mechanism → disease → presentation, not isolated facts. "The better you know something, the fewer words it takes to describe it." Fewer, more-connected cards beat many atomic ones. | **VERIFIED** (yousmle.com/5-keys, /med-school-anki) |
| **Pathophysiologic Chronology (PC)** | For each vignette, "explain EVERY sentence, starting from the day the patient was born." Trace causation as a timeline. Core of both review AND question interpretation. | **VERIFIED** (yousmle.com/how-to-review-uworld, /question-interpretation) |
| **Stand-Alone Question (SAQ)** | Re-phrase the vignette into one question that stands alone without the stem (e.g. complex inferior-STEMI case → "What causes bradycardia in an inferior STEMI?"). Predict the answer BEFORE reading choices. | **VERIFIED** (/question-interpretation) |
| **3-category miss diagnosis** | Every missed Q is: (1) concept gap, (2) recognition gap, (3) unforced error. Fix differs per type — repair content vs build recognition (PC cards) vs read slower/anxiety/burnout. | **VERIFIED** (/how-to-review-uworld) |
| **Two-question screen** | After each miss: "Could I have gotten this with knowledge I already had?" + "What exact behavior would've changed the answer?" No → repair content; Yes → fix behavior. | **VERIFIED** (paraphrased in YouSMLE/related; HIGH confidence) |
| **Forward + reverse cards, ~6/drug** | For pharm: mechanism / use / toxicity, each forward AND reverse ≈ 6 cards/drug. Class-level card for shared properties (e.g. macrolides). "Make more, but shorter cards." | **VERIFIED** (/how-to-master-pharmacology) |
| **Daily card-creation limit** | Cap new cards/day — "the more cards you make, the more you review." Give yourself permission to NOT remember everything. | **VERIFIED** (/5-keys) |
| **Slow is fast** | Spend real time understanding the miss instead of grinding volume. "A QBank improves scores when each question teaches a decision rule," not by volume. | **VERIFIED** |
| **Error LOG (formal template/spreadsheet)** | He teaches error *categorization* but his pages do **NOT** specify a fixed log template or columns. | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — concept yes, template no |
| **"AGAIN/GOOD" grading** | Not in his published Anki pages. This is generic Anki/AnKing terminology. | **UNVERIFIED for Palmerton** |
| **Numeric review cadence (e.g. 6/5/4/3 vueltas)** | He says ~15–20 min/day Anki and "review before you forget," but gives **no fixed pass-count**. Your CRÍTICA=6/ALTA=5/MEDIA=4/BAJA=3 is YOUR ENCAPS layer, not his. | **UNVERIFIED for Palmerton** (compatible, not sourced) |

**Sequencing he endorses (verified):** Do **biochemistry videos first** to build a foundation, then fill gaps via questions, making cards based on what you miss. His sample weekday schedule = early-morning OLD Anki reviews → learn topics → evening finish reviews + master 2–3 topics.

## PART 2 — PER-SYSTEM MINI-METHODS (Palmerton's spirit) `[RECONSTRUCTED]`

Each follows the same skeleton: **Primary free-leaning resource → What to anchor → Question strategy → Common trap.** Anchor logic is always: *mechanism/physiology first, then build the PC timeline to the presentation.*

| # | System | ANCHOR (the one thing) | Question strategy (SAQ form) | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cardio** | Pressure-volume loop + cardiac cycle + baroreceptor reflex. Everything (murmurs, drugs, shock) hangs off preload/afterload/contractility. | "What does this maneuver/drug do to preload or afterload?" Predict murmur change before reading. | Treating murmurs/ECGs as buzzwords instead of deriving from flow/pressure. |
| 2 | **Pulm** | The A–a gradient + V/Q mismatch + the oxygen-hemoglobin curve. | "Is this hypoxia from hypoventilation, shunt, V/Q, or diffusion?" Pick mechanism, then disease. | Memorizing PFT patterns (obstructive vs restrictive) without the flow-volume loop logic. |
| 3 | **GI / Digestive** | The "tube + accessory organs" map: where each secretion/enzyme/hormone acts (gastrin, CCK, secretin) and the portal/biliary anatomy. | "Which segment / which secretion is broken?" Localize anatomically first. | Liver function tests and jaundice without the bilirubin pathway timeline (pre/intra/post-hepatic PC). |
| 4 | **Renal** | The nephron as a sequence: what each segment reabsorbs/secretes + RAAS + acid-base. Most renal = "which transporter, which segment." | "Which nephron segment + which electrolyte/acid-base derangement?" Diuretics map onto segments. | Acid-base questions: skipping the stepwise (pH → primary → compensation → anion gap) algorithm. |
| 5 | **Endocrine** | Every axis as a feedback loop (hypothalamus→pituitary→gland→hormone→negative feedback). Anchor on the loop, not the disease name. | "Where in the axis is the lesion (primary/secondary/tertiary)?" Predict the lab pattern (high/low pairs) before reading. | Confusing primary vs secondary because you memorized diseases instead of deriving feedback labs. |
| 6 | **Repro** | Hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis + the menstrual cycle hormone curves; embryo as a timeline. | "Where in the cycle / which hormone shift?" For DSDs: trace from karyotype → gonad → hormone → phenotype (a perfect PC). | Embryology and intersex without a birth-forward timeline; memorizing rather than deriving. |
| 7 | **Heme/Onc** | RBC indices + the coagulation cascade + cell-lineage tree. Anchor on "where in maturation/cascade is the block." | "Micro/normo/macrocytic? Intrinsic vs extrinsic?" Classify before naming the disease. | Leukemia/lymphoma translocations as flashcards with no lineage/mechanism scaffold. |
| 8 | **MSK / Derm / Connective** | Collagen/elastin + inflammation type; for derm, the histologic layer + lesion morphology vocabulary. | "Which structural protein / which immune mechanism?" | Pure morphology memorization; derm needs the morphology→mechanism link, not picture-matching. |
| 9 | **Neuro** | Neuroanatomic localization (tract + level) + the lesion-to-deficit map. Localize, then name. | "Where is the lesion (which tract, which side, which level)?" before the diagnosis. | Stroke syndromes memorized as lists instead of derived from vascular territory + tract. |
| 10 | **Psych / Behavioral** | DSM time-criteria + neurotransmitter logic + the ethics/communication decision rules. | For ethics/behavioral: "What's the single guiding principle (autonomy, capacity, confidentiality)?" Predict the *best next step*. | Behavioral/ethics treated as memorization — these reward a decision rule, exactly what Palmerton means by "each question teaches a rule." |
| 11 | **Micro** | **Classification scaffold first** (Gram +/−, shape, O2, special features) — treat the chart like a metabolic pathway and draw it. Then layer disease/treatment. | "Classify the bug from the stem, then recall its one defining feature." | Jumping to diseases before the classification chart is automatic. *(Chart-first tip is general best practice, NOT Palmerton-verified.)* |
| 12 | **Pharm** | Mechanism / Use / Toxicity per drug, anchored to the relevant physiology system. Use his ~6-card forward+reverse structure; class card for shared drugs. | "What's the mechanism, and what downstream effect/toxicity follows?" Predict the adverse effect from the mechanism. | Side-effect lists with no mechanistic "why" — fails application questions. **(VERIFIED Palmerton method.)** |
| 13 | **Biochem** | Pathways as connected maps; **draw the pathway from memory** + anchor enzyme defects to presentations. Do biochem FIRST (he says so). | "Which enzyme is missing → what accumulates / what's deficient → what symptom?" (a clean PC). | Memorizing isolated enzymes; integrate with pharm/physio as anchors. *(Draw-from-memory is general best practice; "biochem first" IS Palmerton-verified.)* |
| 14 | **Immuno** | The cell-cascade timeline: innate → antigen presentation → T/B activation → effector. Hypersensitivity by mechanism (I–IV). | "Which cell/molecule is the defect, and at which step of the response?" | Immunodeficiencies as a list instead of mapped onto the cascade step they break. |
| 15 | **Path (general)** | Pathoma Ch. 1–3 mental model (cellular injury, inflammation, neoplasia) applied to EVERY organ. This is the connective tissue of the whole exam. | "Is this reversible/irreversible injury, which inflammation type, benign/malignant criteria?" | Studying organ path without the general-principles lens — the highest-ROI fix on the whole test. |
| 16 | **Biostats** | A fixed decision tree: study design → 2×2 table → which metric. Pure rule-application, no memorization. | "What does the question actually ask for (sens/spec/PPV/RR/OR)?" Compute from the 2×2. | Confusing PPV/NPV with sens/spec under prevalence shifts; not drawing the 2×2 every time. |
