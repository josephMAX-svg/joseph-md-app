## PART 2 — Realistic 8-Week Beginner Ramp (1h/day, weekdays)

**The compounding rationale (why this order):** You are learning two languages at once — **medical English** and **medical content**. They compound: every Pathoma video, QBank explanation, and First Aid page is *also* an English-immersion rep. So the plan **front-loads English + Pathology fundamentals** (which appear in 100% of later questions), so that by Week 3 your reading speed has risen enough that high-weight systems go *faster*. Doing CV/renal in Week 1 would waste them on slow English; doing them in Weeks 4–6 means each minute teaches more. **High-weight systems are deliberately placed AFTER your English ramp, not before.**

Daily 1h template (every weekday) → mirrors your ENCAPS micro-schedule:
- **0–10 min:** Medical-English warmup (read 1 short clinical paragraph aloud; log 5 new terms).
- **10–35 min:** New content (Pathoma/Sketchy video OR First Aid section for the day's system).
- **35–55 min:** **QBank block** — 8–12 questions in **Tutor mode** (Qbankly/AMBOSS), reviewed deeply (Part 3).
- **55–60 min:** Add ~5 cards to AnKing/Qbankly SRS; let SRS schedule vueltas.

| Week | English acquisition (10 min/day) | Content focus (25 min/day) | QBank focus (20 min/day) | Compounding payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Core medical vocab: anatomy + symptom words; read clinical sentences aloud. Free: read [AMBOSS public USMLE pages](https://www.amboss.com/us/usmle/step1) + label diagrams. | **Pathoma Ch. 1–3** (cell injury, inflammation, neoplasia) — the universal pathology base. [Pathoma](https://www.pathoma.com/) | 5–8 easy Qs/day, untimed, *read every word of explanation*. | Builds the path vocabulary every later system reuses. |
| **2** | Lab values + "describing a patient" phrasing (history/PE terms). | **Immune + Heme** (Pathoma Ch. 6–7) + Sketchy Immuno. | 8 Qs/day; start a personal "English+concept" error log. | Immuno is finite — quick win that boosts confidence early. |
| **3** | Pharmacology English (drug suffixes, "-olol/-pril"); read drug monographs aloud. | **Microbiology + Pharm** via [Sketchy free trial](https://www.sketchy.com/explore/medical). | 10 Qs/day; introduce a few **timed** questions. | Memorization-heavy + image-based → forgiving on still-developing English. |
| **4** | **Cardio/renal terminology** — now your reading is faster, tackle dense physio English. | **Cardiovascular + Respiratory/Renal** (Pathoma Ch. 8, 11–12). *High weight.* | 10–12 Qs/day, mixed timed/tutor. | Highest-yield systems hit when English no longer bottlenecks you. |
| **5** | Endocrine + repro vocabulary (axes, feedback language). | **Reproductive & Endocrine** (Pathoma Ch. 13–16). *Highest weight.* | 12 Qs/day; begin mixing systems (interleaving). | Top-weight system placed at peak reading speed = max ROI. |
| **6** | Neuro + psych English (localization terms, DSM phrasing). | **Behavioral + Nervous/Special Senses** (Pathoma Ch. 17) + neuroanatomy. | 12 Qs/day; system-mixed blocks. | Behavioral Qs are English-reading-heavy — now you can parse them. |
| **7** | GI + MSK/derm vocab. **Derm gets extra love (Mayo path).** | **GI + MSK/Skin** (Pathoma Ch. 9–10, 14). | 12 Qs/day; **all timed**, mixed systems. | Lower-weight systems are fast now; derm doubles as research-career investment. |
| **8** | Test-day English: question-stem reading drills under time. | **Biostats block** + **weak-system re-pass** (use Qbankly analytics to pick). | **Full mixed timed sets**; redo flagged Qs. | Biostats = cheapest points; analytics-driven review = ENCAPS "rampa escalante" finish. |

> **Weekday-only note:** 8 weeks × 5 days = 40 sessions ≈ 40h. This is a **foundation ramp**, not a full dedicated period. After Week 8, the same engine repeats system cycles with rising QBank %. Your ENCAPS deadlines + "vueltas hechas" tracker map directly onto this.

## PART 3 — How to Review a QBank Question Properly (max learning)

This is where 80% of QBank value lives. **Doing questions ≠ learning; reviewing them = learning.** Use this loop in Qbankly/AMBOSS/UWorld-style banks:

1. **Mode = Tutor + Untimed (Weeks 1–3), then Timed (Week 4+).** Beginners read explanations immediately while the reasoning is fresh.
2. **Before revealing:** write one sentence — *"I chose B because ___."* Forces commitment; surfaces *why* you were wrong, not just *that* you were.
3. **Read the ENTIRE explanation — including why the 4 wrong answers are wrong.** On Step 1, the distractors are themselves testable facts. This is ~4x the learning per question.
4. **Separate the two failure types** (critical for you):
   - **Content gap** → "I didn't know the mechanism." → make an AnKing/Qbankly card.
   - **Language gap** → "I misread the stem / didn't know the word." → log the term in your medical-English list. *Tracking these separately is the single most important habit for an IMG learning English.*
5. **Extract the teaching point in YOUR words** (1 line, in English) → annotate into **First Aid** as the single source of truth ([how-to](https://www.usmle.org/exam-resources/step-1-materials/step-1-content-outline-and-specifications)). First Aid = "the map," QBank fills its margins.
6. **Flag + tag** every missed Q by system/priority. Feed wrong Qs into SRS so they recur on your **ENCAPS vuelta schedule** (CRÍTICA topics → 6 passes, etc.).
7. **Weekly:** use the bank's analytics (Qbankly's 3PL-IRT predictor, AMBOSS's strength map) to pick the **lowest %-correct system** as next week's re-pass — this is your "rampa escalante de simulacros."
8. **Re-do flagged questions** at least once before exam — a missed-then-mastered question teaches more than a fresh correct one.

> **Beginner rule:** quality ≫ quantity. 10 deeply-reviewed questions/day > 40 rushed ones. The numeric targets in Part 1 are *cumulative over months*, not daily.
