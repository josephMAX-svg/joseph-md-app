## 1. PALMERTON'S CORE METHOD (faithful synthesis)

His entire system reduces to **two goals**, quoted verbatim:
> 1. *"Learn to apply/integrate information useful to Step 1/medical practice."*
> 2. *"Remember that information forever."*

Everything else is machinery serving those two goals.

### 1.1 Philosophy: Mastery over memorization
- *"No Step 1 study plan can fully overcome two years of memorization in two months."* The core failure mode is memorizing without understanding.
- Step 1 questions test **application of concepts, not recall of minutiae** (he leans on the NBME's own item-writing rules). So you must be able to **reason through questions you've never seen before** — memorizing answers to seen questions is a trap: *"If you've seen a question – particularly recently – it's easy to remember the answer without remembering the reasoning."*
- Permission to be selective: *"if I knew roughly 30 to 40% of the information from lecture, but knew it exceedingly well…I would do just fine."* (Depth > breadth.)

### 1.2 The two requirements to get a question right (his framework)
1. **Mastery, retention, and application of the concept** (e.g., "I have mastered microcytic anemias").
2. **Proper recognition** of that concept in a vignette (e.g., "I can recognize α- vs β-thalassemia from the vignette").
A miss is always a failure of one or both.

### 1.3 Error types — the heart of error-logging (VERIFIED, his exact 3 categories)
Every missed question falls into one of three buckets, and **each has a different fix**:

| # | Error type | What it means | The fix |
|---|-----------|---------------|---------|
| 1 | **Lack of concept mastery** | You memorized instead of understood (e.g., preload/afterload in sepsis) | *"Slow is fast."* Stop and understand the **why**; rebuild the concept from mechanism |
| 2 | **Lack of recognition** | The concept was known but you didn't connect the vignette's clues to it (e.g., aortic aneurysm → recurrent laryngeal nerve compression) | Make a **Pathophysiologic Chronology (PC) card** that explains *every sentence* of the vignette |
| 3 | **Unforced errors** | *"Reading too quickly and/or carelessly"* — missed "INCREASE," misread a value | Slow down; treat as an attention/anxiety/burnout signal, not a knowledge gap |

> The discipline: **for every miss, label which of the three it is before doing anything else.** This is "error-correction done right" in Palmerton's terms. This is what your "is my error-correction right?" coaching page should grade against (see §6).

### 1.4 Question-first learning (VERIFIED)
- **"UWorld = Diagnosis; First Aid = Treatment."** Do questions to **find** your gaps; use First Aid/resources to **fill** them.
- *"Doing QBank questions before you've studied the material will likely lead to lower percentages. But does that matter?"* — No. Low % early is a feature: it's a precise diagnostic of what to learn.
- First Aid is *"answers to remembered USMLE questions,"* a **target list of what to master**, not a teaching text.

### 1.5 Anki usage — his 5 keys (VERIFIED)
1. **Pathogenesis-to-Presentation cards.** Connect facts into one causal chain instead of isolated cards. His example collapsed 8 subdural/epidural cards into 2. *"If you can connect the various facts together, you can dramatically reduce the number of cards."* The signature technique — the **PC card** — summarizes a vignette as a causal arrow chain, e.g.:
   > *"RCA plaque rupture → inferior STEMI + SA node ischemia → bradycardia → cerebral hypoperfusion."*
   And in detail it *"explains EVERY sentence in the vignette, starting from the day the patient was born."* This single technique is what he credits for **236 → 270**.
2. **First Aid as priority filter** (high-yield first; supplement with Goljan path, Costanzo physio).
3. **Study during downtime** — mobile Anki in 5–15 min chunks. *"You would be surprised how many cards you can finish."*
4. **Limit new cards/day** — *"More cards created means exponentially more reviews."* (Crucial for your 1h budget.)
5. **Accept imperfection** — learn the high-yield 30–40% exceedingly well.

### 1.6 Review cadence & mindset (VERIFIED)
- **Never skip reviews:** *"I never slept before finishing my reviews."* He ran 20,000+ total reviews while keeping **<100 due at any time** — proof that disciplined daily clearing prevents the dreaded backlog.
- **Effectiveness > rigid schedule:** the governing question is *"Is my studying working?"* He prefers **daily minimums** over hour-by-hour plans because *"you don't know what you don't know, nor how long it will take to learn it."*
- **Untimed questions** if the exam is >1.5 months away (deeper learning); switch to timed only near the end.
- **Mindset stack** (from his 10 habits): deep work over long hours; **9 PM bedtime**; mindfulness/meditation; internet blockers; *"Chase one rabbit, catch it. Chase two, catch neither"* (single-focus); daily journaling to surface stressors — *"Ignorance of your stressors can derail even the best Step 1 study plan"*; teaching others to cement understanding.

**Could NOT verify:** a named numeric interval system (his public writing emphasizes Anki's default algorithm + "clear all reviews daily," not custom day-intervals). Your ENCAPS priority-interval model (1-3-7-28-63) is **compatible** with his philosophy but is *your* system, not a documented Palmerton spec — keep that attribution honest in the app.

**Design principles (Palmerton-faithful, adapted to 1h):**
- Question-first from week 1, but in **tutor/untimed mode** (exam is far off).
- **Hard cap new Anki cards** so reviews never explode in a 1h budget: **start 5/day, never exceed 10/day** this phase.
- Every block ends by **labeling each miss with error type 1/2/3** and making **one PC card** for type-2 misses only.
- English is a *parallel* gain: you learn medical English *through* the questions, not separately.

**The 60-minute daily template (your compounding engine):**
- **0–12 min — Anki reviews** (clear ALL due; this is non-negotiable, the compounding core).
- **12–37 min — Questions** (start 5/day → 10/day), **untimed, tutor mode**, read the *full* explanation.
- **37–52 min — Learn + make cards:** for each miss, write the error-type label; make 1 PC card per type-2 miss; look up 3–5 unknown English medical terms.
- **52–60 min — One concept video** (high-yield system of the week, see ramp).

> ROI note: 1h/day × 5 days = 5h/week. In 8 weeks that's ~40h — enough to build the *habit engine and a 150–250-card high-yield seed deck*, NOT to finish content. The win is **compounding** (a permanent review habit + understanding-style cards), not coverage.

## 4. UWORLD / QBANK ROLE + HOW TO REVIEW PROPERLY

**Role:** UWorld is the **gold-standard diagnostic and the primary learning tool** — not a final-week test simulator. *"UWorld = Diagnosis; First Aid = Treatment."* Do it **untimed, tutor mode** until ~6 weeks out. Quality over quantity: he ran **20–40 q/day** early, building strong cards, *not* blasting volume.

**Palmerton's review protocol (the part that actually moves scores):**
1. **Diagnose WHY you missed it** — assign error type **1 (concept), 2 (recognition), or 3 (unforced)** *before* re-reading the explanation.
2. **Type 1 (concept):** *"slow is fast"* — go to the mechanism, ask **"why?"** repeatedly until the chain is airtight. Rebuild, don't re-memorize.
3. **Type 2 (recognition):** make a **PC (Pathophysiologic Chronology) card** that *explains every sentence of the vignette* as a causal chain (summary arrow-chain + detailed version).
4. **Type 3 (unforced):** no card needed — log it as an attention/anxiety pattern; read slower; check burnout/sleep.
5. **Review the questions you got RIGHT for the wrong reason** too — getting it right by luck is a hidden type-1 error.
6. **Time budget:** review *takes longer than the questions* early on — that's correct. A handful of deeply-reviewed questions beats 40 skimmed.

**Anti-pattern he explicitly warns against:** redoing UWorld to chase a high % — you'll *"remember the answer without remembering the reasoning."* Reset/fresh questions test reasoning; repeats test memory.
