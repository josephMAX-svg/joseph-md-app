## 1. "Why Successful People Struggle in Med School (and How to Fix It)"

### 1.1 Tesis y mecanismo
- Ser inteligente, trabajador o gran memorizador no basta ante el volumen de medicina; el estudiante brillante cae en el **Peter Principle** académico: asciende hasta su nivel de incompetencia porque "what got you here will not necessarily get you there" (Why Successful People Struggle in Med School).
- Diferencia con ciencias duras: "in a hard undergrad science course, I might spend an hour on a single page of my textbook. In med school, spending an hour on one page would be suicide... it is impossible to know everything" (Why Successful People Struggle; High Yield Study Mastery: Identify Key Info for USMLE Success). Alec, químico, recuerda "an hour going over one page of my tiny PM textbook".
- El "bucle de aprendizaje" ineficaz: fallar una pregunta → re-estudiar el tema entero (8 Signs That You're Studying Wrong; I Studied 30,660 Hours).
- El mismo patrón en el dentista: "we see a lot of people who have compensated... because they either were very smart and/or they worked very hard... they were succeeding despite their approach, not because of it" (How a Busy Dentist Makes Time to Study for the USMLE).

### 1.2 Los tres casos (nombres cambiados por privacidad)
1. **"The Golden Child of academics"** (Princeton → Harvard Med; "he could memorize huge amounts of information and he had an excellent recall of facts and details"): "I don't understand how UWorld can contradict itself" — diverticulitis con antibióticos IV en una pregunta y quirófano inmediato en otra; memorizaba asociaciones ciegas sin los parámetros (severidad) que cambian el manejo; "he was struggling with application" (Why Successful People Struggle).
2. **El amigo admitido sin MCAT** (becas prestigiosas, conoció "luminaries", entró a medicina sin MCAT): "he never really learned how to study effectively because he was so smart"; casi reprueba; revelación: **"being smart wasn't enough"**; su corrección fue también "learning how to work harder": "he would go to bed early and he would wake up early and when he did that he would always do better" — Alec lo copió (Why Successful People Struggle).
3. **El MD/PhD** (top-2 med school, campeón nacional de debate, Soros Fellow, varias primeras autorías en Nature): email en crisis, NBME **205-210** ("very below average in the pre-Step 1 P/F era you wanted at least a 240 and ideally 250" → "3 standard deviations below"), consideraba dejar medicina y bajaba de especialidad. Causa: "he was really good at depth but he wasn't good at breadth"; alternaba "PhD mode" (rabbit holes) con bloques masivos en piloto automático. Corrección: aprender bien cada cosa, no olvidarla (spaced repetition) y aplicarla — "filling the bucket... fixed the leaks in his bucket". Resultado: **255 en Step 2** y eligiendo entre Stanford y Harvard "for his dream residency, not the one that he had lowered his heights for" (Why Successful People Struggle).

### 1.3 Técnica accionable
1. Equilibrio estricto **amplitud/profundidad** (disciplina de tiempo por tema).
2. Identificar y romper el "learn more loop".
3. Ante el estancamiento, no culpar al intelecto sino **desglosar el fallo en 3 categorías**: aprendizaje del concepto / retención / lectura crítica de la pregunta ("Bad Test-Taker" Is a Lie; Why Tutoring Doesn't Move Your USMLE Score).
- Anécdota paralela **"Dina Do-Everything"** (Ivy League top-3 con honores → Harvard Med; "her gift was that whatever she saw others do, she could figure out a way to do it better through hard work and will-power"; medía el éxito por el cansancio; colapsó en Step 1 y rotaciones) (How Your Step 1 Prep Influences the Rest of Your Career).

## 2. "The USMLE Mindset That Guarantees a 260+" (sesión en vivo, verificada contra transcripción íntegra) y la familia "250+ / unforced errors"

### 2.1 La pregunta que cambia
- "260 plus on the USMLEs has nothing to do with studying more. One of our students jumped from **225 to 257 in 2 weeks** without memorizing a single new medical fact. The real difference comes down to mindset"; top scorers no preguntan **"What else can I learn?"** sino **"Where am I weakest and how can I fix it?"**; "they focus on small details like test taking skills, question interpretation and strategy" (The USMLE Mindset That Guarantees a 260+).
- Techo del conocimiento: "knowledge will only get you so far, probably to 220s or 230s... How does one go from 220s or 230s to 240s/250s/260s? You have to reduce your unforced errors by interpreting questions properly" (The Secret to Scoring 250/260+ You Can Learn Right Now: Question Interpretation; What 250+ on Step 1 or Step 2 CK Means, and How to Do It).
- Literal del cierre: "That's why people get stuck in the 220s 230s 240s... what got you here isn't what's going to get you there. The thing that people typically ride to get to the 230-240 range is typically knowledge... 'I'm doing all the same things but my score is stuck'... you fixed these problems but you haven't fixed these ones... **the knowledge, the attention, that stuff is not your bottleneck anymore. Your bottleneck is your mindset, your resilience, your consistency**" (The USMLE Mindset).

### 2.2 El modelo probabilístico
- Bloque de 10: fáciles ≈80%, medianas ≈60%, difíciles ≈40%; "the goal is to increase your chances of getting a question correct for every single question, especially the easy and medium ones"; "if you can get those hard ones to 50/50, that's what a 260 looks like... choose the answer that feels better, not perfect" (How to Score USMLE 260+ (Even If You're Failing); The Step 2 Trap Between Him and an Ophtho Match).
- "The game is not binary... it's what's the likelihood that I get the question right and then systematically doing things that will increase the likelihood... **every percent is worth about two points**" (The USMLE Mindset — transcripción [148]).
- Literal de la sesión: "it's like playing many many hands of blackjack or taking lots of three-pointers: you're not going to get every single one, but the more tweaks you make in the right direction the greater the likelihood the shots go in... **every question has a certain probability associated with you getting it correct; all you're trying to do is shift those odds systematically in your favor**"; el pánico "doesn't mean you're guaranteed to get it wrong but it is systematically a lower chance"; "the only reason that people that are really well-prepared don't do well is because they're not able to consistently bring that preparation to bear on every question" (The USMLE Mindset).
- Analogía del tirador: mejorar 8% absoluto de efectividad en tiros = élite (How to Score USMLE 260+ (Even If You're Failing)).

## 3. "'Bad Test-Taker' Is a Lie You Tell Yourself" — skills, not identity

- "If you're a bad test taker there's literally nothing that you can do about it. It's something that's wrong with you. It's unfixable. But if you have bad test taking skills that's something that you can improve" ("Bad Test-Taker" Is a Lie).
- Tres causas raíz distintas para cada fallo: (1) **aprendizaje** (cramming sin entender / hay que reaprender), (2) **memoria** (lo aprendí y lo olvidé), (3) **interpretación** (sabía y aun así fallé) — "completely different root causes for different missed questions" ("Bad Test-Taker" Is a Lie; Why Tutoring Doesn't Move Your USMLE Score).
- "We've had people master this skill and literally go from the bottom 3% to the top 3% on some of the most difficult exams in the world" ("Bad Test-Taker" Is a Lie).
- Amigo de Stanford (Johns Hopkins neurocirugía): "every word has a purpose... it's your job to figure out what the purpose of each word was when the test writer wrote it"; estudio de novatos vs expertos en física (Chi et al.) ("Bad Test-Taker" Is a Lie).
- La "mentira protectora": creer "soy mal test-taker" impide buscar técnicas; compensar con esfuerzo "does work... to an extent" y por eso la creencia se refuerza — ver §10 (I Failed the SAT in 2002).
- "There is nothing wrong with you. You're not stupid. You're not a bad test taker. You're not unteachable. Instead you probably have a skills gap. And remember all skills are learnable. None of us were born being able to walk" (Why Studying Harder Won't Fix Your USMLE Score).
- **Reencuadre de la identidad en Cassie** (literal): ella dice "I don't do well with the 'what's the best answer'"; Alec: "let's be careful, you're describing a symptom as if it's an identity flaw... I'd rather focus on the cause and the skill gap: **'I have not yet gotten good at the emotional discomfort of having a not-perfect answer that I have to choose as the correct answer'**" (Stuck Below 220 on Step 2).
- **Miriam, "coiled spring"** (She Went From Bottom 1% to Top 3% in 2 Months): dentista, >10 años sin ser estudiante, bottom 1% → top 3% en 2 meses "using the exact same materials and being the exact same person"; tres cerrojos: (1) **nivel de detalle equivocado** (entendía amiloidosis cardíaca y fallaba por "a very specific protein" → analogía SAT/vocabulario → **shopping list**; en 10 días salió del bottom 1% "she didn't learn more and she didn't spend more time studying"); (2) **sólo podía responder en una dirección** (ortopnea: nombre→definición sí, definición→mecanismo no → **reverse cards** y no regalar la respuesta en el frente: "epidural hematoma: use the pathophysiology to explain how fast it presents"); (3) **peleaba la batalla equivocada** (6 semanas estancada en 70-71% aunque cada subtema daba ≥80% → "if you don't get your goal score it's not going to be because of knowledge, it's going to be because you panicked on test day"; 5 años creyendo "not smart enough" tras cambiar de país → **EMDR + práctica en condiciones reales** → 83% tres semanas después; "her husband called her the Michael Jordan of exams"; "the most important realization she had was that she was never a bad test taker").

## 4. TDAH: "6 Signs of ADHD I Missed as a High Achiever" + "ADHD Superpowers: 5 Weaknesses That Drove My Success"

### 4.1 Contexto
- "I was the valedictorian of my high school, graduated from Stanford Medical School and completed my anesthesiology residency at Harvard but yesterday I learned that I have ADHD" (a los 39; "it confirmed all my worst fears and insecurities"); disclaimer: no es profesional de salud mental (6 Signs of ADHD; ADHD Superpowers).
- **[CORREGIDO — verificado]** Test de función ejecutiva (artículo, anterior al diagnóstico formal): "One of the things I have [new students] do is take a **test of executive function that doubles as a screen for ADHD**. I took it recently and was surprised to find that I am **borderline ADHD – in the 90%ile or above in a couple of measures of inattention or impulsivity**"; "in the students I coach, a fair number have undiagnosed ADHD... it is helpful to seek professional assessment" (How to Stop Med School Procrastination and Get Work Done Now). En el mismo artículo: "I am a master procrastinator. I am also a recovering perfectionist with borderline ADHD".
- "I have really bad ADHD... very very very poor impulse control when it comes to digital distractions... I have an addictive personality" (How to Study When You're Too Busy to Study; The 10 Behaviors).
- Dr. Russell Barkley: el TDAH es cuestión de **grado** (bell curve), no de síntomas "positivos" como en esquizofrenia — por eso se lo pasó por alto (6 Signs of ADHD).

### 4.2 Las 6 señales (transcripción íntegra)
1. **Impulsividad / inatención = "hyper attention"**: "whenever I get to a hard Anki flashcard I will impulsively, reflexively just check my email... running away from an uncomfortable situation"; fue "one of the earliest users of applications that would block access to email or the internet"; "it's not really inattention with ADHD but hyper attention... constantly switching" (6 Signs of ADHD).
2. **Medidas extremas / todo-o-nada**: sin smartphone en secundaria, college y casi toda la residencia ("I got a smartphone at the very very end of residency" / "the last 6 months of residency" — 10 Behaviors); eligió vivir en la Orange Line a 20-30 min de MGH para estudiar en el metro con un iPod Touch sin internet; dietas restrictivas por tipo (Atkins/keto) no por cantidad: "if I eat one bite [of birthday cake] I will just keep going... if I have a spoonful of ice cream I can eat the entire container" — "I tend to do better if I can just be extreme" (6 Signs of ADHD).
3. **Hiperfoco / obsesión / adicción**: Starcraft en la era del dial-up con facturas enormes; lo cortó "cold turkey" a fines de secundaria: "once I start playing I will not stop"; el mismo rasgo lo hizo obsesionarse con Anki y con los puntos de tarjetas de crédito (vuelta al mundo en business con su esposa siendo estudiante pobre) (6 Signs of ADHD).
4. **Procrastinación y evitación = fight-or-flight**: "we are wired for survival, we're not really wired for success... whether a lion is charging at us or I'm facing an uncomfortable task or there's a difficult flashcard there is going to be the same physiological response"; déficit de función ejecutiva → "I had a really hard time getting started studying"; soluciones: **bloques de internet programados por la mañana con recursos offline** y **deadlines naturales** (clase a las 9:00 en Stanford → cama temprano, despertar ~6:00 o antes, "I have to get this done by 9:00 a.m."; de noche "my self-control was typically a lot weaker and I had no natural deadlines") (6 Signs of ADHD).
5. **Tics motores / fidgeting**: parpadeo excesivo (más de niño), mover la pierna; "non-specific signs" (6 Signs of ADHD).
6. **Regulación emocional**: los déficits se descubren cuando cambian las exigencias (entrar a medicina, primer hijo, fundar una empresa con task-switching constante vs la rutina fija de la facultad); mantra: **"name it to tame it"** y "giving myself grace" (6 Signs of ADHD).
- Lado positivo: más extrovertido, positivo, alta energía, creatividad y ver conexiones; "Smooth waters don't make skilled sailors" (6 Signs of ADHD).

### 4.3 Los 5 "superpoderes" (rasgo → lado oscuro → superpoder → técnica)
1. **Addictive personality** → videojuegos, comida impulsiva (una papa frita = la bolsa) → **"positive addiction"** (Dr. William Glasser): adicto a aprender, leer, estudiar; "I've channeled that obsession into learning some really valuable things" → técnica: **eliminar, no moderar** (sin videojuegos, sin TV, sin chatarra en casa; Kindle junto a la cama y el teléfono fuera del cuarto: "instead of reaching for my phone when I'm restless I will reach for a book") (ADHD Superpowers).
2. **Scattered thinking** → "that's so random", dificultad para explicar cómo llegó a conclusiones → **pensamiento divergente** (vs convergente neurotípico): ver conexiones que otros no ven — ejemplo del profesor de patología en Stanford: "these are lymphoid cells, first sign of acute organ transplant rejection" → "well duh, that's what the immune system always does" — un principio bajo cientos de detalles → técnica: capturar la conexión en Anki (ADHD Superpowers).
3. **Impulsivity** → decir/hacer tonterías cansado, con hambre o con alcohol → **"the flip side of impulsivity is courage... decisiveness, the ability to take action without overthinking"** **[CORREGIDO — verificado]**: "on a whim I applied for a **Fulbright Grant to live in South Korea** after college; one of my friends had done it the year before and said that it was challenging but also very meaningful and so I was like 'sure, sign me up'... as a Korean adoptee I connected with my heritage and my family, learned a completely different language and culture"; "starting a business was a combination of my impulsive nature along with my addictive tendency: I basically got the idea and then obsessively figured out everything that I needed to do and **just did it all within like a weekend**" → técnicas: **no decidir nada importante cansado/estresado/con "big feelings"**; filtrar impulsos con una persona de confianza ("if they're like 'yeah that's great, do it' then I can jump in with even more enthusiasm") (ADHD Superpowers). Nota: el artículo No Motivation? confirma que **difirió la admisión a medicina dos años** por la Fulbright.
4. **High energy** → no puede quedarse sentado tras cenar, se levanta y camina en conversaciones ("natural intensity that can sometimes be a bit overwhelming") → **hiperfoco sostenido por horas** → técnica: **trocear en chunks donde se vea éxito** ("if I needed to learn everything about the heart I might choose only coronary artery disease and see myself get 90%... when I see that success it just fuels that energy") (ADHD Superpowers).

## 12. PROCRASTINACIÓN — modelo, coaching calls y protocolo

### 12.1 El modelo
- **Fight-or-flight**: la amenaza psicológica (un bloque difícil, un NBME bajo, una tarjeta incómoda, una pila de 2.000 tarjetas vencidas) activa la misma respuesta que un león; "flight" = extraerse de la situación = procrastinar / doom-scroll (6 Signs of ADHD; The 10 Behaviors; Top USMLE Mistakes to Avoid; Too Busy — "the size of it made me freeze, it was triggering this fight or flight response").
- **Síntoma vs causa**: "I'm losing confidence / I don't have time / I'm not an Anki person / maybe I'm just lazy" son síntomas de "I'm not seeing good results when I study"; "you can't fix a problem that you can't see" (48% NBME; How to Score 260+ on USMLEs in 2026).
- **El test de Alec**: cuando el método funciona, "when they tell themselves they're going to take a 5-minute break they actually take a 5-minute break as opposed to before where it might stretch to a couple hours"; no cambió nada de su "motivación" (The 10 Behaviors).
- **Perfeccionismo y todo-o-nada** como barrera principal: "Step number six is just start small" (How to Guarantee a Step 1 Pass (Proven System)); "the hardest part isn't doing the work, it's starting the work" (9 Habits).
- **Mastery como remedio**: "Mastery – and learning to enjoy learning again – is an ideal remedy for procrastination. The more excited you are to learn, the more you will study" (How to Stop Med School Procrastination).
- **Procrastinación como enfermedad crónica** (literal): "Procrastination is more like living with a chronic illness we have to manage. Unlike simple bacterial pneumonia we can treat and eliminate, procrastination is with us for the long haul... The key is to remember that we are procrastinators and plan accordingly. If, instead, you blame yourself for your lack of self-discipline and vow to 'be better tomorrow,' you're inviting disappointment" (How to Stop Med School Procrastination).
- **Shrink what you're looking at**: ante cualquier pila (2.000 tarjetas, 1.000 emails, un cuarto desordenado) "when the pile gets big enough you're just going to stop being able to look at it... don't look at the whole pile, just set a small target and get that to zero" (Too Busy).

## 14. IMPLICACIONES PARA EL PLAN v5.7 (lectura para la síntesis, no prescripción)

- El plan mide por "% ciego" (coherente con "where am I weakest"); falta la **hoja de micro-destrezas** por pregunta (contexto/cronología/severidad/SAQ/rule-in/abogado/ghosts/emocional/conocimiento) y la **regla del tercio** como disparador para cambiar de adquisición a interpretación; y una **shopping list** por oración del enunciado tras cada bloque.
- **Franja 04:15-05:45 de IA + Anki 05:00**: Palmerton pone lo más demandante antes de la primera obligación; la coexistencia es válida si la IA no roba la ventana prefrontal del USMLE; el brick nocturno (20:30) y 8 h de sueño son condición del método.
- **Ingeniería de entorno** no está codificada en el plan: candidatos — Freedom/bloqueo por horario en la app, teléfono fuera del cuarto, "No Zero Days" con mínimo (1 pregunta / 10 tarjetas / 1 tarjeta buena) para los días de slip en vez de correr todo el plan (el remap +1 día por slip choca con "no zero days"); regla del backlog (cap 200, nuevas 0) para el Anki tras cualquier pausa.
- **AVP / affect labeling**, **reframe pánico→excitación**, **worst-case rehearsal** y **EMDR** no existen en el plan; Palmerton los prescribe ante cambio de respuestas correctas, evitación de bloques o ansiedad de examen — deberían ser un protocolo de contingencia en el registro de errores, con la matriz "confié/acerté" como métrica.
- **Motivación intrínseca**: el "por qué" (choice/meaning/competence) y el chunk de competencia diaria (un subtema a 80%) son el antídoto de Palmerton contra el abandono; el pre-test 10Q + 30Q del día ya dan la "competence" si se leen como victoria diaria ("80% en 2-3 bloques").
- **Comparación social / ENCAPS**: el patrón de Jamie y Cassie (comparación, coartada del ego) es análogo al historial ENCAPS; la respuesta de Palmerton es data (80% hoy) no ánimo, y "compare yourself to previous versions of yourself: 1% better".
- **Descomposición tipo Ohtani**: el objetivo Step 1 puede desglosarse en componentes (conocimiento por sistema · retención · lectura CCSN · rule-in/out · regulación emocional · consistencia) con sub-metas medibles — el motor del plan sólo mide el primero.
